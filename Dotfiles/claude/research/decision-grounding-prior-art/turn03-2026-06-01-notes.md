# turn 3 — 2026-06-01

Bucket-(b) wave 2: LLM reasoning/verification patterns + the agent-skills ecosystem (the most direct prior art).

## Findings (lifted, one-line)
- LLM reasoning patterns do NOT confer epistemic rigor via self-verification; their selection signal is
  self-referential except where an EXTERNAL environment/test is wired in:
  - ReAct (B): the only one with a genuine external anchor (real retrieval/environment) — but no pre-commit, no adversary.
  - Reflexion (C+): external-anchored ONLY when the Evaluator is a real compiler/unit-test; else self-critique;
    paper admits "relying on the power of the LLM's self-evaluation … not having a formal guarantee for success."
  - CoVe (C): "the model is used to check its own work"; verification is POST-HOC on an already-drafted answer
    (the inverse of pre-registration). Its one useful trick = "factored" context-decorrelation (answer the
    verification Qs without seeing the draft) — but same model, same priors.
  - Self-Consistency (D): consistency ≠ correctness; authors concede "the language model is not well calibrated
    and thus cannot distinguish well between correct solutions and wrong solutions."
  - ToT (C−): LLM self-evaluation steering; wins ride on intrinsically-checkable toy tasks (Game of 24).
- Potemkin Understanding (A, arXiv 2506.21521): models define concepts correctly 94.2% of the time but APPLY
  them wrongly (potemkin rate 0.40–0.55), and show 22% incoherence with their OWN prior outputs → direct
  empirical proof that self-verification cannot certify correctness. This is the theoretical backbone of the
  form-vs-substance acid test.
- Leaderboard Illusion (A−, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2504.20879): hard evidence of Goodhart/benchmark-gaming
  (undisclosed private testing, selective score retraction, data-access asymmetry; +112% gains from
  arena-specific overfitting). "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."
- AGENT-SKILLS ECOSYSTEM (the closest prior art to what we're building):
  - Anthropic's own Skills guidance says ESSENTIALLY NOTHING about epistemic rigor. Closest: "build evaluations
    first" (validates the skill artifact, not the decision it grounds) + "feedback loops (validator→fix→repeat)".
    The official exemplar analysis skill ("Research synthesis workflow") is a bare 5-step prose checklist —
    i.e., Anthropic's house style for analysis skills IS the prose scaffold the acid test condemns.
  - TWO SPECIES exist, and NO published artifact unifies them:
    1. Pre-commitment/falsification: `mythos` (A−, nearest neighbor overall), `idea-evaluation` (B, cleanest
       pre-registered kill-thresholds), `solve` (B−), superpowers `systematic-debugging` (C+).
    2. Adversarial/red-team: `red-teaming-analysis` (B+, full IC/Heuer canon), `LLM Council` (C, anonymized
       peer review), superpowers `requesting-code-review` (C, context-isolated sub-agent).
  - DESIGN-PROPERTY PRIOR ART (key result):
    · (A) refuse-by-question-type → NO precedent found (novel). Skills gate on missing inputs / task-phase /
      trivial triggers, never on the epistemic TYPE of the question.
    · (B) separate-context, user-pasted adversary → NO precedent found (novel). Every independence mechanism is
      IN-SESSION (sub-agents, anonymized peer review). Nearest principle: superpowers' reviewer "should never
      inherit your session's context" — but programmatic sub-agent, not a human-run fresh window.
  - OUR NOVELTY = integration (anchor + pre-commit + INDEPENDENT adversary in one domain-agnostic artifact) +
    the two properties above. The constituent moves each have partial prior art → we must CREDIT mythos /
    idea-evaluation / red-teaming-analysis, not claim invention.
- SELF-CRITIQUE sharpened: the nearest prior art (`mythos`, `red-teaming-analysis`) ports intelligence-community
  tradecraft (ACH, KAC, Devil's Advocacy) — the very tradition RAND/Chang reportedly find lacks empirical proof
  of bias-reduction (A2 subagent verifying). So our skill risks porting moves from an empirically-unvalidated
  tradition. Headline-grade.
